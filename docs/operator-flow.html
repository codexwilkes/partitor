partitor 0.4.0

From score to parts

How to turn a score PDF into large-print parts for your ensemble. You work on your phone or PC over SSH; partitor runs on your partitor host and keeps everything in the library folder (local, or on a NAS). Only the build takes a long time, so you make every change first, review it, and build once.

  1. 1DropPDF into the inbox
  2. 2Analyseproof of who plays what, 1–5 min
  3. 3Editnames, choices, groups, layout
  4. 4Reviewone screen, every setting
  5. 5Buildthe part PDFs, minutes

Dashed: the steps that take time. You can go back from Review to Edit as often as you like; nothing is printed until you build.

Before you start

What you need

  • An SSH app on your phone or PC, logged in to your partitor host as a user that can use sudo. Run partitor with sudo if you are not root.
  • Access to the library folder (see STORAGE-LOCAL.md or STORAGE-SMB.md): a folder you can open with your file manager, SFTP or a NAS app. It has two folders: inbox and library.
  • A score PDF: a vector PDF (from notation software) or a scan. Scans at 300 dpi greyscale work best.

One command. Type partitor and press Enter. Everything else is a menu: you answer with a number or a letter. Enter accepts the value shown in [brackets]; q stops and keeps what is done.

Stage 1

Drop the PDF in the inbox

  1. Copy the score PDF into the inbox folder of the library.
  2. On the partitor host, run partitor. The menu shows the pieces newest first, each under its own fixed number (the serial of its id: 12 is 2026-10-0012, the same number the commands take) and n new from inbox (1 waiting). With many pieces, the five changed last come first as A–E; * marks a piece with work pending. /Bach shows only matching pieces (composer, title, part), / shows all again, + and - turn the page.
  3. Press n, then the number of the PDF, then the pages of the piece (Enter for all pages, or a range such as 8-9 when the PDF holds several pieces).

The PDF moves out of the inbox into a new folder in the library, named by its id, for example 2026-10-0001 (year, month, number).

Stage 2 · takes 1–5 minutes

Analyse and check the proof

partitor reads every page: staves, systems, barlines, bar numbers, staff labels. It does not make any parts yet. When it finishes, open the proof in the item folder:

library/2026-10-0001-bach-example-piece/2026-10-0001-proof.pdf
  • Each page of the proof is a page of the score.
  • Each system has its name in blue, for example p8.s2 (page 8, system 2).
  • Each staff shows the part it goes to. Follow one part down the pages to check that it continues without jumps.

Red frame. A system in a red frame is one partitor is not sure of. Its staves show ? name, the suggestion. You answer these in stage 3.

If the analysis itself is wrong (wrong pages, a staff missed), you can run it again later with y in the item menu, or partitor analyse ID. A failed analysis also leaves a proof, with the problem marked in red.

Stage 3

Edit: names, choices, groups, layout

The wizard asks these in order. Every answer is saved at once but nothing is built, so you can stop with q and come back later.

Title and composer

Title, then the composer's full name (printed on page 1), then a short form for the head of the inside pages (suggested: the surname). Change them later with m.

Part names

The names come from the printed staff labels. Change one at a time, then Enter when done:

TypeDoes
7=Organrenames part 7
7=-no PDF for part 7; it stays in the list, marked “not extracted”
7=+makes the PDF for part 7 again
7+8joins part 7 into part 8: one part on two staves (a keyboard)
7=-; 8=Continuoseveral in one line: basso continuo only, from a keyboard part

Choices for the red systems

For each uncertain system the wizard shows where it is (source page, system from the top, proof page), its clefs and labels, and the suggestion as part numbers. Answer with one part number per staff, top staff first:

  [1/9] p8.s2: source page 8, system 2 of 4 from the top (proof page 8)
     3 staves, clefs top to bottom: F4 G2 F4
     suggestion: Bass Trombone, Continuo, Continuo  (= 6 7 7)
  part number for each of the 3 staves, top first (Enter = suggestion;
  0 = leave out; same number twice = one part on two staves): 6 7 7
  • Enter takes the suggestion.
  • A corrected answer becomes the suggestion for the next systems with the same clefs: press Enter there.
  • 0 leaves a staff out (a staff you left out shows as “(left out)” and 0 in the suggestion); the same number twice puts one part on two staves.
  • q stops the questions; continue later with r in the item menu.
  • The proof is drawn again after each answer.

Bar numbers

When a piece starts with a short bar (a pickup) or a repeat splits a bar, the wizard asks Y/n. Enter accepts. Check a few numbers against the proof. Correct later with n: p 1 pickup on or off, s 22 1 bar 1 of line 22 completes the bar before it (a half bar over a line break; both halves keep one number), f 3=16 line 3 starts at bar 16, j 4 1 join a false barline, b 4 list the bars of line 4. The proof shows the barline ticks, the bar numbers (magenta) and the rehearsal letters (boxed): press v, open the proof, and press Enter to save. partitor proof 12 draws it again from the saved file.

Groups (optional)

A group prints several parts together as a small score, for example the four trombones. Press n at “Build now?”, then g in the item menu, and type Trombones=2,4,5,6 (part numbers). Trombones= deletes the group. The group becomes one more PDF; staves of other parts leave no gap, and each staff is labelled.

Open it any time: partitor, then the item's number. The status line lists what has changed since the last build.

Stage 4

Review everything

Press v in the item menu (or partitor show ID). The wizard also shows this screen before it asks “Build now?”.

2026-10-0001 · Example piece · 7 parts · ready to build status: ready to build title: Example piece composer: J. S. Bach short: Bach (from the composer) parts: 1 Canto (G2) — ambitus E4-G5 2 Alto Trombone (C3) — ambitus F3-A4 4 Tenor Trombone I (C4) — ambitus C3-F4 (by hand) 7 Continuo (G2+F4) — two staves, ambitus none (off) group: Trombones: Alto Trombone, Tenor Trombone I, … bars: line starts 1, 3, 6, 9 …; letters A (p10.s3) settings: paper=letter

Check the parts list, the groups and the title. If something is wrong, go back to stage 3; nothing has been built yet.

Ambitus. Each part's lowest and highest note, printed in place of the part name at the start of the first line (in a group score, one per staff), as two stacked black noteheads at 80 % size, as in engravings. Every note counts, so check it: the proof rings each part's lowest and highest note in green, with its bar, page and system; show and k give the bars too, and a doubtful value is marked ? and asked at the end of the analysis, with a suggested range without the doubtful note (Enter takes it, k keeps the value as read). A wrong value is fixed with k (or s and Alto Trombone:ambitus=F3-A4); =auto reads it again from the notes, =off prints none. Continuo parts have none unless =on.

Stage 5 · takes minutes

Build and print

Press r. partitor reads the pages again and writes one PDF per part, one per group, and a large-print copy of each. A long score takes several minutes (the pages are read by several workers at once); other services on the host keep running (the build runs at low priority).

To change one part (a new gap, a corrected ambitus), edit it, then press r followed by its name or number, for example r Trombones or r 2 4. Only those parts are written again; the other files in parts are not touched. If a name in the list would change (title, composer, part number), partitor builds all parts and says why.

The parts are in the item's parts folder. The name starts with the part so a player finds it first:

06-bass-trombone-Bach-Example--2026-10-0001.pdf
06-bass-trombone-Bach-Example--2026-10-0001-11x17.pdf
08-trombones-Bach-Example--2026-10-0001.pdf
  • Normal copy: Letter (or A4), printed double-sided. Page turns fall at movement starts, new sections or rests; a section that fits on two pages stands on a facing pair.
  • Large-print copy (-11x17): print on 11×17 (ledger) paper at 100 %, then cut at the short crop marks. A4 scores give an -A3 copy instead, no cutting. Paper ledger gives no large copy: the sheet is 11×17 already.
  • Ledger (paper=ledger, menu a): 17×11 in, landscape, for a larger staff (up to 8 mm; Letter holds 3.6–3.8 mm on long lines). The lines are 2.2 times as wide, so there are about twice as many pages. Print double-sided, flip on the short edge (the 11 in side, like a book). Stands for two pages side by side are wide: check the stand first. The staff is as large as the page allows (up to 8 mm) and the pages follow. To fit more systems on a page, set Trombones:group_gap=5 (staff spaces between the staves of a group; also a part on two staves) or gap=7 (space between lines).

Small print. When the score's lines are long, the staff can come out small. partitor then offers re-flow (experimental, off by default; for a larger staff use paper=ledger): lines cut at barlines with the clef copied, for a staff up to 8 mm, on more pages. It shows the size and pages before and after; you keep it or not. A group (for example the trombones) is cut only at barlines that all its staves have, and stays within 1.5 times its pages unless you set pages. The build prints a re-flow: line: how many lines can be cut and why the others stay whole.

When something goes wrong

Common problems

You seeDo
“library folder not reachable”The library folder or the NAS is not reachable. Check it, wait a minute, run partitor again. See STORAGE-SMB.md.
The wizard stopped (q, lost connection)The item is kept. Open it from the menu and continue; r asks what is still open.
A part is missing on some linesCheck the red systems on the proof; correct with t or analyse again with y.
Bar numbers are off from some line onn, then f N=B for the first wrong line.
A part rests for whole systemsNormal: those bars become one rest line with the count (for example 14), so the numbers continue.
